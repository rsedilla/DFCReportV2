# 2026-09-09 — A DCC coverage gap names the leaders who owe a record, within scope

Section 9 makes a leader who has not submitted "a reporting gap, not a cancelled service", and
says those "are tracked as coverage". That makes the gap a **number**. Nothing said whether a
screen may name *which* leader it is.

For Cells the equivalent is already required: Section 15's attention list surfaces "Cells with
meetings still awaiting a record" to the leader who oversees them, and Section 7 names
"outstanding-record lists" among what `cell.*` guards. The DCC half had no counterpart.

**A DCC coverage figure may be drilled into, and the drill-down names the responsible leaders
within the actor's scope who owe a record for that event.** It is an attention list on Section
15's terms.

## Why naming rather than counting

**A number nobody can act on is what Section 19 warns against**: "a dashboard of counts tells a
leader nothing to act on", which is why that section puts outstanding work above the figures.
Section 15 says the same of its own lists — "each entry offers the actions that resolve it".
`7 of 8` tells a leader something is missing and withholds the one fact that would let them
fix it.

**The act it enables already exists.** Section 14 permits an upline leader to record on behalf
of a downline leader within their pastoral subtree, and `dcc.submit_on_behalf` is the
capability for it. A list of who owes a record is the surface that capability was always going
to need; without it, recording on behalf requires knowing by other means who has not recorded.

**Nothing new is disclosed.** Every roster line already carries `responsible_leader_id`, and
decision 0194 settled that the DCC roster publishes per-person figures by design, "because a
leader marking a checklist must see who is already marked". Naming a leader who owes a record
for an event is the same fact one level up, over people the actor may already see.

## The scope is the whole of the constraint

**The list covers the actor's own pastoral subtree and never the church**, unless the actor's
grant is church-wide. This is the line that matters, and it is not a matter of taste: the same
data shown church-wide and ordered by how many records are missing is a leaderboard, which
Section 13 forbids "as a default or landing view" and forbids comparing leaders who do not
oversee one another.

So: **filtered, never ranked, never colour-graded** (Section 15). Ordered by name or by date,
never by how far behind somebody is. It states who owes a record and offers the action that
resolves it; it does not say who is worst.

## What was rejected

**Leaving it as a number.** It is the status quo and it makes Section 19's own instruction
unfollowable for the DCC half — outstanding work cannot lead a dashboard if the outstanding
work cannot be named.

**A church-wide list for every holder of the viewing capability.** It converts an attention
list into the ranking Section 13 exists to prevent, and it does so without anybody deciding to,
which is how such a thing usually arrives.

## What this does not settle

**Whether a leader may record their own DCC attendance**, which is recorded as open in
`CLAUDE.md` and which this list touches only in that it makes the actor's own outstanding
record visible to them.

**Nothing about Cells**, whose attention list Section 15 already requires and which needs no
ruling — only building.
