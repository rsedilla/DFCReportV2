# 2026-10-01 — An administrator disables and re-enables an account from the person page

Section 6 defines what disabling an account does and makes reactivation a separate
decision, and nothing could set either: an account given to the wrong person, or to a leader
who left, could not be switched off. Asked on 2026-10-01, before leaders encode the church,
the owner approved the person-page sketch and settled three questions Section 6 left open.

## The ruling

**1. An administrator holding `accounts.manage` disables an account, and re-enables a
disabled one, from the person page.** Each asks before it acts. No reason is asked for,
since Sections 6 and 21 ask for none.

**2. Disabling ends every session at once and kills every activation and reset link.**
The holder is cut off at their next request and cannot sign in. Their roles, grants and
records are untouched.

**3. Nobody disables their own account.** Whoever disables somebody is then an
administrator who can still sign in, so the church is never left without one, and no rule
about the last administrator is needed.

**4. Any other account may be disabled, a Senior Pastor's or an administrator's
included.** A Senior Pastor keeps their role and seat while disabled, so no third person
can take the seat, and re-enabling gives it back.

**5. Re-enabling returns an account to `ACTIVE` where its holder has set a password, and to
`PENDING_ACTIVATION` where they never did.** It ends sessions and kills links again, so
nothing from before the disablement works after it; the administrator resends the activation
email from the same page.

## Why

Disablement is an authentication decision (Section 10): it stops the holder signing in and
leaves their authority alone. So the two actions change one thing, whether the account can
be used, and nothing a report or a pastoral record depends on. Refusing one's own account
answers the lockout question with one check rather than a count of administrators, because
the actor is necessarily an administrator still able to sign in.
