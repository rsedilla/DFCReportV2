# 2026-09-23 — Growth reads answer as things are now

Section 7 names exactly three capabilities that resolve as of the period being viewed —
`cell.view_subtree`, `reports.view_subtree` and `audit.view` — and says every other
capability resolves as a write, in a closed form written so that "a capability added later
cannot fall between the rules". Sections 27 and 28 then added three more viewing
capabilities, `conquest.view_subtree`, `suynl.view_subtree` and `training.view_subtree`,
and said nothing about which side they fall on. `CLAUDE.md` carried that as a Stop
Condition from the day those sections were written. It is settled now because the modules
are being built and the next reader would otherwise derive the answer; it is not reachable
even now, there being no route and no screen (Sections 27 and 28).

The owner chose this answer from a drawing of both readings, applied to one disciple who
left a leader's branch between two months.

## The ruling

**The three Growth viewing capabilities resolve as of now**, by Section 7's default, and
the closed list stays at three.

**A person who leaves an actor's pastoral subtree leaves that actor's Growth screens with
them.** Their lessons, graduations and confirmations stay on their own record, where their
new leader reads them.

## Why

**Their screens name no period, so there is no period to resolve at.** Section 28 requires
the counts to be stated "as of now", and a dated resolution answers a question nothing on
the screen asks. Section 7's dated rule exists for a viewer asking about a past month; a
reader asking what somebody has done is asking about today.

**It changes no behaviour, and that is why it is cheap to settle now.** Section 7 already
answers an undated viewing request at now, and nothing in either section names a period, so
the two readings are indistinguishable until a route names one. What the ruling fixes is
which answer arrives when one does: a period-naming Growth route cannot inherit a dated
resolution from this class, and would need Section 7's closed list amended and a ruling of
its own.

**The default already decides these three; the ruling records which answer it gives.**
Section 7's closed list exists so that a capability appearing in neither of two open lists
still has an answer. Writing it down costs nothing and saves the next reader a derivation.

## What it does not decide

Whether a Growth figure may ever be reported by period, which Section 28 already records as
open and which is what would make these dates effective dates in Section 3's sense.

**Which capability would guard such a figure**, and therefore which instant applies to it.
Sections 27 and 28 both say a count asked for beyond one actor's own tab is governed by
Section 20, whose authorization instant is dated; this ruling resolves the Growth read
capabilities at now. Nothing says whether that route would carry a Growth read capability or
`reports.view_subtree`, and the two answers differ. Recorded as open by the review of this
ruling rather than decided in it.

**What Section 7 means by a *viewing* capability**, as against a *read* capability. Section
7's dated rule is keyed on the first word and its list of eight is headed by the second, and
this ruling is the first place both classes are named in one sentence. The wording here
keeps them apart — these three are read capabilities and never viewing ones — which is a
convention rather than the definition Section 7 owes. Recorded as open by the same review.
