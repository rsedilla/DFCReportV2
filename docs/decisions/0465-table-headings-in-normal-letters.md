# 2026-11-10 — Table headings in normal letters, and the Cells table's shorter

Every table's heading row was small grey capitals with wide letter spacing (decision 0423). On a
sideways iPad the Cells table cut its *Members* heading off at the column's edge, and *Meetings
Recorded* took two lines. The owner asked for shorter headings, was shown what design systems do
(1 to 3 words naming what the column holds, never cut off, numbers right-aligned, and headings in
normal letters in GitHub's Primer, Material Design and GOV.UK), then this table today beside two
proposals and four other tables today beside normal letters, all on a sideways iPad, and chose
normal letters for every table.

## The ruling

1. **A table's headings are bold grey words in normal letters on the pale grey**, at the size of
   the body text's small print, with no added letter spacing, on every table. The header row stays
   light, as decision 0423 made it; only the capitals and the spacing go.
2. **The Cells table's headings are *Cell*, *Leader*, *When*, *Members* and *Recorded***, and its
   phone cards read *When* for the day and time. *When* names what the column holds, a day and a
   time; *Recorded* stands beside *2 of 4*, which already says it counts meetings.
3. **No table heading is cut off at any width the application is checked at.** The layout check
   that scans every screen at each width now fails on a heading wider than its column, because a
   clipped heading does not make the page scroll and nothing else could see it.

What a heading says on any other table is unchanged. *Recording Progress*'s tables already had
headings in normal letters (decision 0432), so every table now matches them.

## Why

Spaced capitals are wider than the same word in normal letters, so a heading needed more room than
its column had on a sideways iPad, and a long one took two lines. Normal letters fit, read more
calmly, and match the design systems the owner looked at.

Considered and rejected: shorter words in capitals, which still cut *Members* off on a sideways
iPad and would have needed its column widened too; and changing the Cells table alone, which would
have left it looking unlike every other table.
