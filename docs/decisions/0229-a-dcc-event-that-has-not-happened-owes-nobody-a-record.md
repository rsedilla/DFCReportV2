# 2026-09-09 — A DCC event that has not happened owes nobody a record

Section 9 defines a coverage gap for one case and states the display rule for another.
The gap is "a leader who has not yet submitted their people's attendance for **an event
that did take place**". The display rule, written with the events index on 2026-09-09,
says "a removed event carries the removal and no coverage, because nobody owes a record
for a service that was not held".

A **future** Sunday satisfies the first sentence's negation and is not named by the
second. The calendar runs thirteen months ahead (Section 9), so the index lists one, and
the first build gave it a real figure: `0 of 8`.

**An event whose Manila day has not begun carries no coverage and names nobody as owing a
record, exactly as a removed one does.**

## Why this rather than a zero

`0 of 8` is not a neutral statement about a future service. It says eight leaders have
failed to record it. That is the accusation Section 13 exists to prevent — "ranking the
measure destroys the measure" — arriving as arithmetic rather than as a status, and
reaching Section 15's attention list, which the drill-down puts leaders' names on.

It also breaks the guarantee Section 15 makes of that list: "each entry offers the actions
that resolve it". Nothing resolves a record for a service that has not happened. The
roster refuses the submission on the same ground, so a leader following the list from the
figure to the act would meet a refusal the figure had invited.

## Why the event is still listed

Section 9 wants the calendar visible: the events index exists so a leader can reach a
Sunday's roster, and a month view shows a **removed** Sunday "in its place, as a decision
somebody took" rather than sliding past a gap. Both arguments are about seeing the month
whole, and both apply to a Sunday that has not arrived. So the row is present, says it is
not recordable and why, and carries no figure — which is the shape the removed case
already had.

## A closed month is the opposite case and keeps its figure

`MONTH_CLOSED` is not in this rule. The obligations were real, the window has shut on
them, and the figure is the frozen record of what was and was not filed — which Sections
13 and 20 require a report covering that month to keep showing. Withholding it would hide
the month the window closed on, which is the one month a leader most needs to read.

## What was rejected

**Refusing the whole month**, as Section 20 does for a report naming a period that has not
begun (decision 0216). A DCC month is a calendar as well as a figure, and refusing it
would take away the roster route's only index for every Sunday of that month. The Cell
listing takes the opposite answer for the opposite reason — its rows carry nothing but a
Cell and a figure — and that asymmetry is deliberate rather than an oversight.

## What this does not settle

**What `open` means for a month that has not begun.** Section 20 says Section 17's flag
"cannot correct" a period that has not started, because it distinguishes a month still
being recorded from one that has shut and says nothing about one that has not begun. The
events index answers `open: true` for such a month. That is recorded as open in
`CLAUDE.md` rather than settled here, because it binds every route carrying the flag and
not this rule alone.

**Nothing about Cell coverage.** A Cell's denominator is a schedule count, derived rather
than owed, and its route refuses a month that has not begun instead. Which of the two
answers a *list* should give is the Stop Condition recorded in `CLAUDE.md`.
